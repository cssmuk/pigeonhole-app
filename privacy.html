Privacy Policy

Pigeonhole · Last updated November 10, 2026

Pigeonhole is a personal tool that its owner runs on their own computer to organize their own email. This policy explains what it does with mailbox data.

What Pigeonhole reads

With the owner’s permission, Pigeonhole reads message headers (such as sender, recipients, subject, and date), labels or folders, and, only when archiving a year the owner has approved, the full messages.

How the data is used

  • Only to organize the owner’s own mailbox: filing mail into folders, moving mail from senders the owner chose to a junk folder, and building encrypted yearly archives that the owner approves.
  • All processing happens on the owner’s own computer.
  • Pigeonhole never sends mail, never permanently deletes mail, and never clicks links or unsubscribe links.

What is shared

Nothing. Mailbox data is never sent to the developer, to any AI or analytics service, or to any other third party. Pigeonhole contacts only the owner’s own email providers. It has no telemetry and no advertising.

What is stored

Sign-in tokens, the owner’s sorting decisions, a local activity log, and encrypted archives are stored on the owner’s own computer, readable only by the owner. Nothing is stored anywhere else.

Google user data

Pigeonhole’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access

Access can be removed at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Deleting Pigeonhole’s folders on the owner’s computer removes everything it stored.
